The town that runs on one shared garden

Nobody owns the plot on Bream Street. Forty households take turns, and the rules are written in chalk on a board by the gate.

HK
Hana Kowalski · 14 min read · 3 November

The chalkboard by the gate lists names, not rules. Tomasz has Wednesday mornings. Mira and her son have Saturday afternoons. A line at the bottom reads, in careful handwriting, "leave the hose coiled."

It started as a fight over a strip of grass nobody could be bothered to mow. Twelve years later the same strip grows tomatoes for most of the street, and the fight is remembered as the best thing that happened to it.

What the board actually does

Anyone can write their name. Anyone can cross out somebody else's, but only with a reason written beside it. The board is the whole system — no committee, no minutes, no treasurer. If your reason is bad, the next person writes theirs underneath.

"We tried a group chat for a month. The board won because you can't mute a board."

When I asked whether the arrangement had ever collapsed, three people gave the same answer in different words: it collapses every spring, and every spring somebody re-chalks it.
